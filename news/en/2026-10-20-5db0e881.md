# Double silver for Switzerland: national teams impress in Saarbrücken

*20 Oct. | Written by Hannes Wallimann*

Two teams reached the final and all three national teams made the podium: Switzerland put in a strong performance at the Leonhart World Series. There were further medals in the youth events and the over-63 doubles, while Samantha Di Paolo finished fourth twice.

The Swiss seniors fell just one goal short. At 29–29 in the semi-final against Belgium, they had a place in the final within reach. Gilles Perrin and Roger Ganz fought back in the deciding doubles match and brought the contest back into play, but Belgium scored the winning goal. Switzerland responded in the bronze-medal match, beating the USA 30–23 to secure a place on the podium.

In the nations event, two singles matches are followed by a deciding doubles match. Goals carry over between matches, and the first team to reach 30 goals wins. Individual performances matter, but so does what the team can achieve together.

Switzerland was represented by 95 players at the Leonhart World Series in Saarbrücken from 14 to 18 October. The delegation collected medals and came close to reaching the finals in several events.

### Women turn around their semi-final; men follow suit

The Swiss women showed how to recover from a deficit. They fell behind Austria in the semi-final before Beatrice Berner regained the lead. Cindy Kubiatowicz and Samantha Di Paolo sealed the doubles win, 30–22, to reach the final.

Switzerland took the lead in the final too. Nicole Krüsi put the team ahead against Germany, but the hosts turned the match around and claimed the title. The Swiss women took silver.

The men finished in the same position. After wins against Serbia, the Netherlands and Luxembourg, they faced France in the final. The teams were separated by just one goal before the last doubles match. France pulled away in the final stretch, winning 30–25.

### Di Paolo and Kubiatowicz challenge the eventual champions

The women's doubles event showed how close Switzerland came to another final. Samantha Di Paolo and Cindy Kubiatowicz fought back after losing a set in the quarter-final and then took the lead in the semi-final. Against Belgium's Ellen Van Onckelen and Bulgaria's Ekaterina Atanasova, they needed just one more set at 2–1 to reach the final, but the eventual champions turned the match around.

The Swiss pair finished fourth out of 117 teams. Di Paolo also impressed in singles: she beat top seed Djamila Nader of Germany and reached the semi-final. She led 2–1 in the bronze-medal match as well, but Luxembourg's Cindy Da Fonte came back to win. Di Paolo therefore took home two fourth-place finishes as well as her national-team silver.

### Youth and veteran players add to the medal tally

Swiss pairs also reached finals in the age-group events. Rayan Van Stokkum won silver in the U19 Classic doubles with Slovenia's Marcel Petek. Stephan Frieden and Daniel Flüeler also reached the final in the over-63 doubles and finished second.

Lou Zwald added two podium finishes: bronze in the U19 girls' singles and silver with Germany's Milena Heiert in the girls' Classic event.

Switzerland returned from Saarbrücken with five silver and two bronze medals. The national teams were the highlight: twice in the final and on the podium in all three events. One more goal would nearly have sent the seniors to the final as well. It was Switzerland's most successful appearance at a Leonhart World Series to date.
