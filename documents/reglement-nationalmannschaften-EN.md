## 1. Introduction

The national teams are the figurehead of the Swiss Tablesoccer Federation (STF). They represent Swiss table football at home and abroad and contribute significantly to the perception and development of the sport.

This special role means that all national team players are expected to display a high standard of sporting conduct, fairness, and integrity. This applies both during competition and in their conduct outside official engagements.

This regulation establishes clear guardrails for the organization and leadership of the national teams. At the same time, it serves as guidance for players and as support for coaches and officials. The focus is deliberately not on detailed regulation, but on creating a transparent and comprehensible framework that enables development while ensuring clarity.

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this regulation is to define the fundamental structures, responsibilities, and processes relating to the STF's national teams. It aims to create transparency, clarify the roles of those involved, and ensure a fair and comprehensible organization.

The regulation deliberately avoids rigid selection criteria and leaves room for sporting judgment. Instead, guardrails are defined that structure the decision-making process and make it comprehensible.

This regulation applies to all national teams of the STF, in particular the women's, men's, and seniors' categories. Additional provisions may be established for juniors and Disabled categories. This document applies to all international engagements, World Cups, World Series, and other international team competitions.

### 1.2 Legal Basis

This regulation is based on the statutes and regulations of the STF, as well as on the requirements of the International Table Soccer Federation (ITSF). In addition, the Ethics Charter in Swiss Sport and the provisions on anti-doping and integrity apply.

In the event of contradictions, the higher-ranking regulations take precedence.

### 1.3 Definitions

For the purposes of this regulation, the following terms apply:

- **Activity** - Regular participation in national and/or international tournament play.
- **A-Squad** - Players with current priority for international engagements and a central role.
- **B-Squad** - Players with development potential who can be built up for future engagements.
- **Captain** - National team player with sporting leadership responsibility during a competition.
- **Coach (National Coach)** - Person responsible for the sporting leadership, development, selection, and nomination of the national team.
- **Doubles Compatibility** - Playing, tactical, and communicative alignment between two players, particularly with regard to well-rehearsed doubles pairings (i.e., doubles engagements).
- **Multi-Table Ability** - The ability of a player to be competitive on different types of tables and to adapt flexibly to different playing conditions.
- **National Team** - Corresponds to the A-Squad.
- **Nomination** - Final determination of the players for a specific international engagement.
- **Selection** - The process of choosing players for a squad or a competition.
- **Scouting Squad** - An extended circle of players observed by the coach.
- **Player Pool** - The total of all players potentially relevant for selection within the tournament scene.
- **Sporting-Sensible Team Structure** - A team composition that enables competitive and strategically sensible participation in a competition.
- **Team Spirit** - The ability to contribute constructively to a team, to take on responsibility, and to act in the interest of the team's success.
- **Availability** - The willingness and ability of a player to take part in training, preparatory measures, and competitions of the national team.
- **Performance Sport Board (VL)** - Person within the STF Board with strategic responsibility for performance sport and the national teams.

### 1.4 Basic Principles

The leadership and selection of the national teams follow clearly defined basic principles. At the center are the principle of performance, fairness towards all players, and the transparency and traceability of decisions.

A central element is team orientation. What matters is not necessarily the individual strength of a particular player, but rather which composition offers the best prospects of success for the team as a whole. Success is measured not only by ranking, but also by the achievement of predefined goals, which may also include other aspects.

The national teams should be both sporting successful and contribute to the sustainable development of table football in Switzerland.

## 2. Organization and Responsibilities

### 2.1 Performance Sport Board (VL)

The Performance Sport Board (VL) is part of the STF Board and bears overall strategic responsibility for the national teams. It ensures that the organizational framework conditions are in place and that the basic principles of this regulation are upheld.

Its tasks include, in particular, ensuring a fair and ethically sound environment, as well as responsibility for budget and long-term direction. The VL also participates in defining the goals of the national teams and supports their implementation.

The VL has the right to develop proposals for filling the coach position and to submit them to the Board. It may also veto personnel decisions in the event of serious concerns, particularly regarding conduct or integrity.

Direct influence on sporting selection or the team line-up in competition is expressly excluded.

#### 2.1.1 Requirements

- Understanding of national and international table football
- Experience in strategic and organizational matters
- Acting in the interest of the association as a whole (no individual interests)

#### 2.1.2 Responsibilities

- Overall strategic responsibility for the national teams
- Ensuring organizational and financial framework conditions
- Co-defining the goals of the national teams
- Monitoring compliance with fairness, ethics, and transparency
- Budget responsibility
- Preparing the election for the coach position

#### 2.1.3 Duties

- Ensuring a fair and ethically sound environment
- Supporting and accompanying the coach at a strategic level
- Monitoring compliance with the regulation
- Participating in planning and goal-setting
- Requesting squad definitions (A/B) from the coach
- Participating in annual planning

#### 2.1.4 Rights

- Proposing coaching candidacies to the STF Board
- Co-determining strategic goals
- Right of veto in the event of serious concerns (e.g., conduct, integrity) regarding a nomination
- Requesting justifications for squad decisions

### 2.2 Coach (National Coach)

The coach is elected by the STF Board and bears overall sporting responsibility for the national team. The term of office generally follows a World Cup cycle.

The coach is responsible for compiling the squads, for the sporting development of the players, and for the preparation and execution of training and competition measures. The coach makes the final decisions regarding selection, nomination, and line-ups in competition.

In doing so, the coach is obliged to base decisions on comprehensible criteria and, if necessary, to justify them transparently. Communication with the players and with the VL is central in this respect.

If the coach is also an active player, particular attention must be paid to transparency and to handling any potential conflicts of interest. In such cases, the VL may assume a supervisory role.

#### 2.2.1 Requirements

- Technical competence in table football on different types of tables
- Competitive experience (national/international preferred), on different types of tables
- Ability to make objective decisions
- Leadership and communication skills
- Social competence and sense of responsibility

#### 2.2.2 Responsibilities

- Overall sporting responsibility for the national team
- Observing and developing the player pool
- Compiling the squads (A/B/scouting)
- Planning and conducting training and preparation
- Ensuring the optimal team composition

#### 2.2.3 Duties

- Making comprehensible and justifiable decisions
- Transparent communication with players and the VL
- Acting in the interest of the team
- Professional leadership of the team
- Mediating conflicts within the team
- Disclosing conflicts of interest

#### 2.2.4 Rights

- Final decision on selection and nomination
- Decision on squad composition
- Appointing the captain
- Determining the line-ups in competition
- Deciding on tournament participation and sporting measures

### 2.3 Captain

The captain is appointed by the coach. The captain must be part of the national team and takes on the sporting leadership of the team during competition.

The captain is responsible for implementing the tactical instructions and supports the coach in leading the team. If the coach is not present at a competition, the captain takes over the coach's operational tasks on site.

#### 2.3.1 Requirements

- Member of the national team (ideally A-Squad)
- Sporting experience
- Leadership strength and decision-making ability under pressure

#### 2.3.2 Responsibilities

- Sporting leadership of the team in competition
- Implementing the coach's tactical instructions
- Supporting team leadership on site
- Taking over operational leadership in the coach's absence

#### 2.3.3 Duties

- Acting in the interest of the team
- Responsible decision-making during competition
- Fostering a positive team dynamic
- Ensuring clear communication within the team
- Stabilizing the team in high-pressure situations

#### 2.3.4 Rights

- Making operational sporting decisions during competition
- Implementing and adapting strategy during the game
- Taking over sporting decision-making authority in the coach's absence

### 2.4 National Team Players

National team players are assigned by the coach to different squads. As a rule, a distinction is made between an A-squad, a B-squad, and an optional scouting squad.

All squad players are expected to fulfill their role-model function, to behave in a sporting and fair manner, and to place the team's interests above individual interests. This also includes active participation in training and team measures, as well as professional conduct towards the public, media, and sponsors.

In return, they are given the opportunity to represent Switzerland internationally and to be part of the national team.

#### 2.4.1 Requirements

- Compliance with ITSF and STF provisions
- Active participation in tournament play
- Consistent, demonstrable sporting performance, both current and over the past 2 years
- Team spirit and willingness to perform
- Availability for training and competitions

#### 2.4.2 Responsibilities

- Representing the STF at home and abroad
- Contributing to the sporting success of the national team
- Promoting a positive team culture
- Supporting the team beyond individual interests
- Serving as a role model in sporting and social conduct

#### 2.4.3 Duties

- Sportsmanlike and respectful conduct
- Compliance with team and association rules
- Participation in training and team measures (as far as possible)
- Professional conduct towards the public and opponents
- Wearing the official team kit
- Early communication in the event of absences or restrictions
- Commitment to the team (no lone-wolf behavior)

#### 2.4.4 Rights

- Participation in international competitions as a national team player
- Fair and transparent treatment in the selection process
- Entitlement to comprehensible communication from the coach
- ~~Receipt of official national team equipment~~
- Use of the title "national team player" (A-Squad)

## 3. Eligibility

For selection, the requirements under the ITSF must be met. In addition, a valid STF license is required.

Players must, in principle, be available for training, preparatory measures, and competitions. Engagements for the national team generally take priority.

Absences or restrictions must be communicated at an early stage. Repeated or short-notice withdrawals without good reason may be taken into account in the selection process.

For selection, in particular, the following requirements must be met:

- Compliance with the ITSF nationality provisions
- Valid STF license
- Eligibility to participate under the association's regulations
- Compliance with ethics and anti-doping provisions
- Availability for training, preparation, and competition
- Impeccable sporting and personal conduct

## 4. Selection System

### 4.1 Principle

Selection is carried out by the coach and is based on objective and subjective criteria. The goal is the best possible team composition, taking into account sporting, tactical, and team-dynamic aspects.

### 4.2 Criteria

Objective criteria include, in particular, results at national and international level, rankings, and activity in tournament play.

Subjective criteria include, among other things, team spirit, doubles compatibility, mental strength, tactical flexibility, current form, and international experience.

Regular participation in tournament play is a prerequisite. Prolonged inactivity may be taken into account during selection.

#### 4.2.1 Objective Criteria

- Playing practice and activity
- Results at national level & results at international level (ITSF)
- Rankings (STF, ITSF)
- Activity and tournament participation
- Direct comparative values

#### 4.2.2 Subjective Criteria

- Team spirit and social competence
- Doubles compatibility and well-rehearsed partnerships
- Tactical versatility
- Mental strength
- Current form
- International experience
- Multi-table ability
- Sporting conduct
- Willingness to commit to the team

### 4.3 Weighting

The weighting of the individual criteria is not fixed and is at the coach's discretion. What matters is that decisions are comprehensible and justified in the interest of the team.

### 4.4 Procedure and Nomination

The selection process includes observing the player pool, evaluating against the defined criteria, and the final decision by the coach.

The final nomination is made by the coach. The VL may only intervene if fundamental principles such as fairness or integrity are violated.

In the event of injury, loss of form, or insufficient availability, the nomination may be adjusted.

## 5. Processes and Collaboration

The coach is elected by the STF Board on the proposal of the VL. After the election, sporting and structural goals are jointly defined.

Regular evaluation takes place during the cycle. This includes both an interim review during the cycle and a final assessment.

In the event that a player is not selected, it is expected that the conversation is first sought directly with the coach. If no clarification can be reached, the VL may be consulted. The final sporting decision rests with the coach.

Conflicts within the team are, in principle, mediated by the coach. The VL may be called upon for support if needed.

## 6. Training and Tournaments

Training, scouting sessions, and team measures are central components of preparation. Participation may be a prerequisite for selection.

The goal is the fullest possible presence at relevant international competitions. The teams are primarily made up of players from the defined squads.

The formation of random or uncoordinated teams should be avoided.

If no coach is available, the captain assumes operational sporting leadership, while the VL ensures organizational responsibility.

If a complete team is not available on site, participation only takes place if a sporting-sensible team structure can be guaranteed.

### 6.1 Tournament Participation

- The goal is presence at all relevant international competitions
- Planning is carried out by the coach and the VL

### 6.2 Training Sessions

- May be organized by the coach and the VL
- Regular training is desired
- Participation may be a prerequisite for selection

## 7. Competition Organization

For international engagements, the rules of the ITSF as well as the internal provisions of the STF apply. Sporting leadership on site lies with the coach, in coordination with the captain.

## 8. Finances and Sponsoring

The financial framework conditions are determined by the STF. Details regarding cost coverage, personal contributions, or compensation are regulated separately.

Sponsoring generated in connection with the national teams benefits them exclusively.

The financial framework conditions are determined by the STF.

- Cost coverage by the association
- Personal contributions from the athletes
- Reimbursements and contributions
Details may be regulated in separate provisions.

The STF may define recognitions or support for the coach.

## 9. Ethics and Integrity

All parties involved undertake to comply with the applicable ethics and integrity guidelines. Respectful conduct, fairness, and a clear role-model function are central elements.

## 10. Disciplinary Measures

In the event of violations of this regulation or of fundamental rules of conduct, measures such as a warning, suspension, or exclusion from the squad may be imposed.

- Warning
- Suspension
- Withdrawal of a nomination
- Exclusion from the squad

## 11. Communication and Publication

The squads are communicated publicly. Direct communication with the national team players is generally conducted via the coach. The VL may be involved if necessary.

## 12. Appeals / Objections

An objection may be raised against selection decisions. The review is limited to compliance with the procedures and basic principles, and does not extend to the sporting assessment.
