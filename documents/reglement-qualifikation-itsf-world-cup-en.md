## 1. Introduction

This document is intended to set out how players can qualify for the WORLD CUP of the International Table Soccer Federation (ITSF) and how the process works.

This document is drafted in German. The German version is the authoritative original, both in wording and in form. Versions in other languages have been produced by machine translation.

## 2. Background

For each ITSF WORLD CUP, the ITSF allocates a certain number of starting places for various events. This number may change from one ITSF WORLD CUP to another, depending, for example, on the number of licensed players, etc.

In addition to the fixed number of wildcards that the Swiss Tablesoccer Federation (STF) can award, it is possible to qualify via an ITSF route. These qualification routes are communicated by the ITSF. Such a qualification via the ITSF — provided the starting place is taken up — thereby increases the quota of players that the STF can report to the ITSF.

## 3. Basic Requirements for Qualification

For all qualifications, it is a prerequisite that eligibility to play at the ITSF WORLD CUP has been granted by both the ITSF and the STF.

All players who qualify via the ITSF or the STF must also play under the Swiss flag, otherwise they lose their starting place.

Participation is only possible in one age category.

## 4. Qualification via the ITSF Route

If players qualify via the ITSF route, the following applies:

- Qualification via an ITSF route takes precedence over the STF route (Swiss Tablesoccer Finals, i.e., the Swiss Championships).
- In doubles events, players must also compete at the ITSF WORLD CUP in the same pairing.
- Players qualified via the ITSF in doubles events who do not take up this starting place may not qualify (with a different partner) via the STF route. Exception to this rule: if a player of the team that qualified via the ITSF route is completely unable to attend the ITSF WORLD CUP (i.e., cannot play any other events either, including for the national team), the remaining player has the option of qualifying with a different partner via the STF route.

## 5. Qualification via the STF Route (Swiss Tablesoccer Finals)

How many players can qualify via the STF route depends on the number of wildcards available to the STF. The number of wildcards varies from one ITSF WORLD CUP to another. The idea is that at least the Swiss champions of the years between the previous ITSF WORLD CUP and the upcoming ITSF WORLD CUP, in the respective events, qualify.

### 5.1 Allocation of Wildcards

For the events at the ITSF WORLD CUP that are decided at the Swiss Tablesoccer Finals (the Swiss Championships, i.e., SC), players or teams qualify depending on the number of wildcards (WC) available.

Specifically, in accordance with the following provisions (example with 3 SCs between two ITSF WORLD CUPS):

| | 2 WC | 3 WC | 4 WC | 5 WC | 6 WC | 7 WC etc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC Year 1 | – | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 … |
| SC Year 2 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 … |
| SC Year 3 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1-3 … |

*Table legend: SC = Swiss Championship; Year 1 = first qualification year, i.e., the SC furthest in time from the WORLD CUP.*

*Graphical representation of the order of wildcard allocation (example with 3 SCs between 2 WORLD CUPs). (The diagram from the original PDF is not reproduced here.)*

- Players who qualify in doubles events must also compete at the ITSF WORLD CUP in the same pairing, otherwise they lose their starting place.
- When registering for the Swiss Championship, a team must decide whether, in the event of a placing at the upcoming SC (Swiss Tablesoccer Finals) sufficient for qualification to the ITSF WORLD CUP, that starting place will be taken up.
- If a team decides, when registering for the SC (Swiss Tablesoccer Finals), to take up the starting place, a starting place earned at an earlier SC in the same event lapses (there is no right of choice / choice of partner).
- If the doubles partner qualifies via the ITSF route with a different player, the remaining player may qualify with a different partner via the ITSF route or the STF route, at a different SC.
- Should a player of the team that qualified via the STF route be completely unable to attend the ITSF WORLD CUP (i.e., cannot play any other events either, including for the national team), the remaining player has the option of qualifying with a different partner via the STF route.

### 5.2 Reserve Rule

- Players or teams may qualify as reserves, for example for the following reasons:
  - A player or a team decides not to take part in the ITSF WORLD CUP, or is unable to attend
  - A player or a team has already qualified at a different SC
  - STF wildcards become available because players or teams have qualified via the ITSF route.
- Reserves are determined, up to and including 4th place, in the following order:
  1. The highest rank not yet qualified across all relevant SCs.
  2. For teams that achieved the same placing: first the best-placed from the most recent SC before the upcoming ITSF WC, then that of the second most recent SC, and so on.
- Should players or teams have moved up as reserves up to and including 4th place at both SCs, and not all wildcards have yet been filled, the further participants are determined by further reserves from the most recent SC, from 5th up to and including 8th place, as follows:
  1. If the further places from 5th place of an event at the Swiss Championship are not contested: 5th-placed finishers move up as reserves based on the highest placing in the STF ranking of the most recent season (in doubles events, the players' points are added together). If the points are exactly equal, the higher number of STS season wins applies; if this is also equal, the higher placing in the corresponding ITSF ranking (likewise added together for doubles events) is decisive. Should further wildcards remain to be allocated even then, these are determined in accordance with Section 8 of this regulation.
  2. If the further places from 5th place of an event at the Swiss Championship are contested: according to the highest placing; in the event of a tie: in the sense of the paragraph above (highest placing in the STF ranking of the most recent season).

## 6. Other Disciplines

### 6.1 Classic Doubles

Should the respective events be contested at the Swiss Championships, players and teams qualify on the basis of the qualification provisions under Sections 4 and 5.

**ITSF Route:**

- Players qualified via the ITSF route in Classic Doubles must also compete at the ITSF WORLD CUP in the same pairing.
- Players qualified via the ITSF route in Classic Doubles who do not compete in that pairing at the ITSF WORLD CUP for the respective event may also not compete if they have (additionally) qualified via the STF route (with a different partner). The sole exception is if a player of the team that qualified via the ITSF is completely unable to attend the ITSF WORLD CUP (i.e., cannot play any other events either, including for the national team): the remaining player then has the option of qualifying with a different partner via the STF route.
- If a player of the team that qualified via the ITSF route is completely unable to attend the ITSF WORLD CUP (i.e., cannot play any other events either, including for the national team), the remaining player has the option of qualifying with a different partner via the STF route.

**STF Route:**

- Qualification via the STF route is based on the results at the SCs in the Women's Doubles and Men's Doubles disciplines. Depending on the number of wildcards, players qualify in accordance with the following table:

| | 2 WC | 3 WC | 4 WC | 5 WC | 6 WC | 7 WC etc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC Year 1 | – | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 … |
| SC Year 2 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 … |
| SC Year 3 | Rank 1 | Rank 1 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1+2 | Rank 1-3 … |

*Table legend: SC = Swiss Championship; Year 1 = first qualification year, i.e., the SC furthest in time from the WORLD CUP.*

- Teams that have qualified via the STF route have the choice of whether to also contest the Classic Doubles discipline in the same pairing.
- When registering for the Swiss Championship, a team must decide whether, in the event of a placing at the upcoming SC sufficient for qualification to the ITSF WORLD CUP, that starting place will be taken up.
- If a team decides, when registering for the SC, to take up the starting place, a starting place earned at an earlier SC in the same event lapses (there is no right of choice / choice of partner).
- If the doubles partner qualifies via the ITSF route with a different player, the remaining player may qualify with a different partner via the ITSF route or the STF route, at a different SC.
- If a team decides, when registering for the SC, to take up the starting place, a starting place earned at an earlier SC in the same event lapses (there is no right of choice / choice of partner). The sole exception is if a player of the team that qualified via the STF route should be completely unable to attend the ITSF WORLD CUP (i.e., cannot play any other events either, including for the national team): the remaining player then has the option of qualifying with a different partner via the STF route.
- Teams must decide by the deadline under Section 7. If the respective teams waive participation in the Classic Doubles discipline, or if further starting places remain to be allocated, the participants are determined in accordance with Section 8.

### 6.2 Junior Doubles / Senior Doubles / Para Competitions and Other Disciplines Not Mentioned in This Regulation

- Should the respective events be contested at the Swiss Tablesoccer Finals (Swiss Championships), players and teams qualify on the basis of the qualification provisions under Sections 4 and 5.
- Should the respective events not be contested at the Swiss Tablesoccer Finals (Swiss Championships), the wildcards are allocated in accordance with Section 8.

## 7. Registration and Withdrawal with the STF, and Nominations

Following the last SC of the qualification period, the player or team has, until the deadline communicated by the STF at the relevant time, the opportunity to register or withdraw definitively for the ITSF WORLD CUP, per event.

It is the responsibility of the players / teams to register or withdraw bindingly within this deadline. If the deadline for registration/withdrawal is missed, the starting places lapse and the corresponding players / teams move up in accordance with Section 5.2, "Reserve Rule". Should further wildcards still be available, these are allocated in accordance with Section 8.

Short-notice withdrawals occurring after the definitive nomination (generally around 30 days after the last SC held before the ITSF WORLD CUP) are handled in accordance with Section 8.

The STF reports the nominations to the ITSF.

## 8. Miscellaneous

For the situations explicitly mentioned, and for any other constellations that arise and are not covered by this regulation, or in the event of short-notice, unavoidable changes, the STF Board decides how to proceed and to whom any (remaining) places are allocated.

For this purpose, the STF Board may appoint a body (e.g., the sports commission) or a person (e.g., the Head of Performance Sport at the STF) to be entrusted with this task.
